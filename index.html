How I Define Interactions

Interaction specs aren't just documentation — they're translation layers. I turn ambiguous "make it feel good" feedback into precise animation timings, easing curves, state transitions, and behavior rules that developers can execute pixel-perfectly.

Arrest Flow Specification

The problem: Players needed to feel the satisfaction of solving a case, but the arrest mechanic had multiple states, animations, and error conditions that could confuse developers or result in janky implementation.

My approach: Created a comprehensive interaction spec defining every micro-moment:

  • Button state transitions (Examine → Arrest → Not Guilty)
  • Trait-matching animation sequence with precise easing curves
  • Success/failure feedback loops (banner animations, fireworks, screen transitions)
  • Tooltip behavior (scale-up timing, auto-dismiss duration, pointer positioning logic)
  • Edge cases: What happens when traits don't match? How do we guide the player without punishing them?

Why this matters: By defining interaction logic this precisely, I eliminated 3 rounds of revision between design and engineering. The shipped version matched the prototype 95% — rare for complex multi-state flows.

Saga Map Art + Interaction Spec

The problem: Standard match-3 home screens are level grids (Level 1, 2, 3...). We needed a story-driven map that communicated narrative state, not just level progress — and it needed to be technically feasible for a small art team.

My approach: Wrote a combined art + interaction spec that defined:

  • Tileable background system (vertical tiling for infinite scroll, perspective rules, Indian street aesthetic)
  • Player indicator behavior (idle bounce after 90s, movement along path, arrow slide-in on level unlock)
  • Node states and animations (locked shake, unlock lock-break, active node pulsating glow)
  • Meta-task attention patterns (first meta after current level bounces every 2 minutes)
  • Background ambient animations (clothes swaying, trees, auto leaving frame)

Why this matters: This wasn't just "make a pretty map." I defined a system that art, animation, and engineering could execute independently but assemble cohesively. The spec bridged three disciplines with one document.
